Career Evidence Pilot

Reads a CV, pulls out what the person actually did, and recommends career areas only when the evidence supports them. A building a hospital app is treated as software work, not as nursing.

Pilot for demonstration. Every CV on this page is invented. The output shown is the real, recorded output of the pipeline (a free AI model, one run per case). It supports career exploration only; it makes no hiring, selection or ability judgments.

Where the project stands

Results on invented test cases whose expected answers were written by the project team and are not yet independently reviewed.

The pipeline in five steps

  1. Read the CV. Split into sections and passages. Contact details are removed. Instructions hidden inside a CV are ignored.
  2. Extract evidence. The AI lists what the person did: role, actions, tools, qualification, and the industry context. The industry is never treated as a profession. Each item must quote the CV exactly.
  3. Assess all 94 career areas in a fixed catalogue, each as supported, industry exposure only, interest only, study in progress or not supported, citing evidence items.
  4. Check. Software, not the AI, rejects citations that do not exist, that are only skills lists or stated interests, that are negated, that are study still in progress, or that do not match a regulated occupation (for example nursing needs a nursing role or qualification).
  5. Rank and report. Only areas that survive the checks are shown as supported. Everything else is labelled, never presented as a recommendation.

Reading the labels

Supported by evidence the CV shows the work or qualification itself.

Supported (transferable) related skills or tasks are shown.

Industry exposure only the person worked in that industry, not in that profession.

Study in progress / stated interest worth exploring, but no experience yet.

Evidence strength 0 to 4 rates how well the text supports an area. It is not a probability of success.
